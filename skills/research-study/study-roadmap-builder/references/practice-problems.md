# Vector Fundamentals — practice problem set

Problem-only delivery: when the user asks for practice, emit one problem and
wait. Do not attach a solution, hint list, or "compare with my solution" block.
Verify after they answer.

## 1. Geometric vector representation

**Problem:** A hiker walks from the trailhead at (2, 5) to the summit at (7, 12).
Describe this walk as a geometric vector: where does it start, where does it
end, what direction does it point.

**Check for:** start point, end point, direction stated in words. A bare "(5, 7)"
is the algebraic form — push for the geometric description.

## 2. Algebraic vector representation

**Problem:** Write the displacement vector for a ship that sails 8 km due south
and then 6 km due east. Give it as a coordinate tuple in R².

**Check for:** (-8, 6) with the sign convention explained (south = negative y).

## 3. Vector addition operations

**Problem:** Three legs of a journey:

- Leg 1: campground (2,3) to lake (7,8)
- Leg 2: lake (7,8) to forest clearing (5,1)

Find the total displacement from the campground to the forest clearing.

**Check for:** (5,5) then (-2,-7), summing to (3,-2). The insight to surface is
that the total depends only on start and end, not the intermediate stop.

## 4. Scalar multiplication rules

**Problem:** Compute:

1. 4·(3,-2)
2. -1·(5,0)
3. (1/2)·(0,8)

And state in one sentence what happens to direction when the scalar is negative.

**Check for:** (12,-8); (-5,0); (0,4). Direction reverses 180° when c < 0.

## 5. Vector magnitude calculation

**Problem:** Find the magnitude of (4,-7,2,3) in R⁴.

**Check for:** √78. Ask why the negatives vanish — squaring kills the sign.

## 6. Unit vector normalization

**Problem:** Find the unit vector in the direction of (8,-6). Then confirm its
length is 1.

**Check for:** (4/5,-3/5); confirmation 16/25 + 9/25 = 1.

## 7. Position vectors in space

**Problem:** Point P is at (3,-2). Give the position vector OP. Then give the
position vector of P relative to the anchor A(1,1).

**Check for:** OP = (3,-2); AP = (2,-3). Same point, different anchor, different
vector — the relativity idea.

## Pure-algebra warm-up (used mid-session)

**Problem:** Solve √(5x²) = 20.

**Check for the reasoning, not just x = ±4√5:** squaring both sides turns the
right side into 8²-style 20², √ and squaring are inverses, and the ± appears
because squaring erases sign. Reject a² ÷ a — exponents do not cancel by
division.

## Delivery notes

- One problem per turn. Do not batch all seven.
- After the answer, confirm briefly and name the one idea the problem was testing.
- If the user's step is wrong, show the operation that would have to hold for
  their step to work rather than only marking it incorrect.
